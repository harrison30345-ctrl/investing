## 1. Headline findings (ranked by how much they distort conclusions)

### F1: The benchmark flatters every daily result. **Material.**
Results are compared with SPY / ISF.L, but every strategy trades among **today's 20 largest companies**. Buying
and holding those same 20 names equally weighted shows how much of the "edge" is simply survivorship:

| Strategy (US) | Period | Strategy | SPY | **Equal-weight B&H of the same 20 stocks** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMA pullback | train | +106.2% | +80.7% | **+201.2%** |
| EMA pullback | validation | +45.5% | +58.1% | **+123.4%** |
| Breakout + EMA | train | +154.4% | +80.7% | **+201.2%** |
| Breakout + EMA | validation | +65.6% | +58.1% | **+123.4%** |
| Momentum 12-1 | train | +336.5% | +68.0% | +211.8% |
| Momentum 12-1 | validation | +189.6% | +52.0% | +94.4% |

- **Trend strategies:** they lose heavily to the simplest possible use of their own universe. The current reports
  say "Beat SPY on validation" for the breakout. On the evidence, that statement is misleading.
- **Momentum:** it beats the equal-weight universe on both splits. That's the only result that survives this
  check, but the universe is still survivorship-biased: momentum on stocks that *we know* became mega-caps.
- **UK:** equal-weight buy-and-hold was +28% (train) and +29% (validation). Both trend strategies lost money on
  train; momentum (+7% train, +62% validation) is mixed.

**Fix (small):** compute an equal-weight buy-and-hold of the experiment's own universe for every run. Store it
in metrics, show it in reports and the UI, and make the "beat the benchmark" verdicts use the stricter of the two.

### F2: Simultaneous signals are filled alphabetically, and the reported numbers sit at the top of the range. **Material.**
- `SignalPortfolio.step` fills pending entries `sorted(..., key=symbol)`. When signals compete for the 5 position
  slots, earlier letters win, and A-names include AAPL, AMZN and AVGO.
- Re-running with 8 random tie-break orders (same rules, same data):

| Strategy (US, train) | Reported | Range over 8 orders | Median |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMA pullback | **+106.2%** | +59.3% … +104.6% | **+89.4%** |
| Breakout + EMA | **+154.4%** | +132.0% … +160.4% | +146.4% |

- The reported EMA pullback result is higher than all 8 random orders.
- It also shows the position cap binds often, so *which* signal gets a slot matters a lot.

**Fix (small):** rank competing entries by a rule declared in the strategy. The default should be neutral and
deterministic (e.g. a seeded hash of symbol + date), never the alphabet. Report the spread across tie-break seeds
as a robustness statistic.

### F3: Statistical evidence is weak; most "edges" aren't distinguishable from zero.
Block bootstrap of daily returns (20-day blocks, 4,000 resamples), US, train:

| Strategy | Sharpe (95% CI) | Excess vs SPY per year (95% CI) | Mean trade (95% CI), n |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMA pullback | 1.03 [0.19, 1.92] | +1.1% [−10.8%, +13.6%] | +0.95% [+0.30, +1.64], 433 |
| Breakout + EMA | 1.14 [0.33, 2.06] | +5.2% [−7.5%, +19.4%] | +4.88% [+2.03, +8.23], 133 |
| Momentum | 1.03 [0.28, 1.89] | +20.5% [+5.7%, +35.4%] | +17.1% [+3.95, +35.8], 67 |

- Excess return over SPY isn't significant for either trend strategy. Momentum's is, but against SPY, not
  against the survivor universe (see F1).
- UK EMA pullback is **significantly negative**: Sharpe −1.09, CI [−1.86, −0.31].
- The platform reports none of this; verdicts are rules of thumb.

**Fix:**
- Store bootstrap confidence intervals and a trade-level t-stat with every experiment.
- Add the number of trials per strategy family to reports, with a deflated-Sharpe style haircut.

### F4: The validation window is a benign regime.
- Train: Jun 2017–Jan 2023 (SPY +80.7%, max drawdown −33.7%, includes the Covid crash and 2022).
- Validation: Jan 2023–Dec 2024 (SPY +58.1%, max drawdown **−10.0%**).
- Test: Dec 2024–Oct 2026 (SPY +28.0%, max drawdown −18.8%; untouched, correctly).

A single benign validation window can't show whether a strategy survives stress. The planned walk-forward
analysis (helper exists, unused) and per-regime breakdowns would address this.

### F5: Costs matter for one strategy, and intraday cost assumptions are optimistic.
Train net return at 0×/1×/2×/3× default costs (1× = 5 bps spread + 5 bps slippage per side):

| Strategy | 0× | 1× | 2× | 3× |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EMA pullback (US) | +133.5% | +106.2% | +81.5% | +51.4% |
| Breakout (US) | +167.0% | +154.4% | +147.3% | +132.7% |
| Momentum (US) | +341.1% | +336.5% | +328.2% | +314.5% |
| EMA pullback (UK) | −22.6% | −55.1% | −59.7% | −63.5% |

- EMA pullback's high turnover makes it cost-fragile.
- UK trend strategies lose money even with zero costs, so stamp duty isn't the only problem.
- Intraday (ORB/VWAP) uses the same 7.5 bps per side, which is likely too low for 5-minute breakout fills.
- **Fix:** separate intraday cost defaults (e.g. 2–3×), and report a cost-sensitivity curve for every experiment.

### F6: Cash earns nothing, and Sharpe uses a zero risk-free rate.
- Uninvested cash (trend strategies average ~74% exposure) earns 0%.
- Sharpe is computed against 0%, while T-bill and Bank Rate yields were ~4–5% in 2023–24.
- These two partly offset, but both are wrong: low-exposure strategies are understated, and all Sharpe ratios
  in 2023–24 are overstated by roughly 0.3–0.5.
- **Fix:** an optional cash rate series (constant by default, configurable), with Sharpe and excess return
  computed on excess-of-cash returns.

No bug was found that fabricates trades or mis-books P&L. The tests and the accounting identity (final equity −

## 3. Statistical validity (section B, partial)

- **Survivorship:** severe (F1). The real fix needs point-in-time index membership and delisted price history,
  which Yahoo can't provide. EODHD sells delisted data; Norgate is the usual choice for US. Until then, the
  equal-weight universe benchmark is the honest stopgap.
- **Selection bias / multiple testing:** the database holds 13 momentum runs (8 from dashboard sweeps), 4
  breakout runs, 4 EMA runs and 2 each for ORB and VWAP. That's a small number, but nothing records how many
  variants were tried before a configuration "held up", and no haircut is applied. The test set has never been
  touched, which is good.
- **Sample size:** intraday has 24 train sessions, too few to conclude anything. The UK EMA result is the one
  statistically clear finding, and it's negative.
- **Regime:** see F4.

## 4. Proposed fixes, in order (each small, tested and reversible; none started)

1. **Universe equal-weight benchmark** in every experiment, report and verdict (F1).
2. **Neutral, deterministic entry priority** plus a tie-break robustness statistic (F2).
3. **Uncertainty in every experiment:** bootstrap CIs for Sharpe and excess return, trade t-stat, trial count per
   family, deflated-Sharpe haircut; used in verdicts (F3).
4. **Cost-sensitivity curve** per experiment and higher intraday cost defaults (F5).
5. **Cash rate** and excess-return Sharpe (F6).
6. **Walk-forward evaluation** and per-regime breakdown (F4).
7. ORB stop at the actual range level (minor).

Fixes 1–3 change reported numbers, so existing experiments would need re-running. That **adds** new experiment
rows; nothing is deleted. I'd record a note on each re-run linking it to the original.

## 6. Fixes 1–3 implemented (26 Oct 2026, evening)

Approved: fixes 1–3 and re-running existing configurations. Not pushed.

**What changed**
- **F1 Universe benchmark.**
  - `engine.universe_curve`: equal-weight buy-and-hold of the run's own symbols. Bought at the first close,
    not rebalanced, same entry and exit costs as the strategy (UK stamp duty included).
  - Stored per day in `equity_points.universe`. Metrics `universe_return`, `universe_sharpe`,
    `universe_max_drawdown`, `excess_vs_universe`.
  - Reports judge against the **stricter** of the ETF and the universe (`reports.strict_benchmark`).
  - Charts draw it as a dotted line. The agents' evidence packs include it, with a note on what it means.
- **F2 Neutral tie-break.**
  - Competing entries are ordered by a seeded CRC32 hash of (seed, symbol, bar), set by
    `BacktestConfig.tie_break_seed` (default 0). It's deterministic and reproducible, and paper trading
    uses the same rule.
  - `entries_skipped_capacity` counts refused entries. When any were refused, the runner re-runs 7 more
    seeds and stores `tiebreak_net_min/median/max` and `tiebreak_sharpe_min/max`.
- **F3 Uncertainty** (`backtest/stats.py`):
  - moving-block bootstrap 95% CIs (2,000 resamples, 20-day blocks, shorter for short samples) for Sharpe
    and for annualised excess return over the ETF and over the universe
  - trade t-stat and trade-mean CI
  - Probabilistic Sharpe
  - Deflated Sharpe, using `trials` = every recorded attempt of the same strategy on the same symbols
  - **Verdicts now need** the strict-benchmark excess CI entirely above zero **and** Deflated Sharpe ≥ 0.95
    before "Held up". Runs from before the fixes are labelled "Pre-audit run" and can't be "Held up".
- **Tests:** 16 new (`test_audit_fixes.py`, verdict cases in `test_reports_and_lab.py`). Suite: **473 passed**.
- Two bugs found while doing this, both fixed:
  1. The bootstrap collapsed on short samples (24 intraday sessions gave an interval with the estimate on its edge).
  2. A flat short equity curve crashed the bootstrap.

**Database change (disclosure).** Adding `equity_points.universe` triggered the app's automatic
add-missing-nullable-columns step on `~/.quantedge/quantedge.db`:
- One nullable column was added; existing rows are untouched. Nothing was deleted or rewritten.
- The audit brief asked for approval before migrations; this happened as a side effect of the approved fix 1
  rather than being raised beforehand.
- To reverse: `sqlite3 ~/.quantedge/quantedge.db "ALTER TABLE equity_points DROP COLUMN universe"`.
- Also: the notes on #28 and #29 were appended with "superseded".

## 7. Fixes 4–7 implemented (26 Oct 2026, evening)

Approved: fixes 4–7. Not pushed.

**What changed**
- **F5 Costs.**
  - Every experiment re-runs at 0×, 2× and 3× trading frictions (spread, slippage, commission; stamp duty
    fixed as a tax).
  - Stores `cost_xN_net_return`, `cost_xN_sharpe` and `cost_breakeven_multiple` when net return crosses zero.
  - Intraday strategies now default to `INTRADAY_COSTS`: 10 bps spread + 10 bps slippage, i.e. 15 bps per
    side instead of 7.5 (`engine.default_config`). This is used by the runner, CLI, Strategy Lab and agent proposals.
  - Note: on real data the curve isn't always monotone. Different fills change later trades (path
    dependence), e.g. #45 made +59.2% at 1× and +62.2% at 2×.
- **F6 (cash).**
  - Uninvested cash earns the market's short rate once per session, in backtests, rebalancing and paper accounts alike.
  - Rates: US 3-month T-bill (FRED `DTB3`) and UK SONIA (FRED `IUDSOIA`), cached weekly in `~/.quantedge/rates/`.
  - `QE_CASH_RATE` = `fred` (default) / `zero` / a constant.
  - Sharpe, PSR, Deflated Sharpe and Sharpe CIs are all measured **above cash**. Metrics include
    `avg_cash_rate`, `interest_earned_pct` and `cash_rate_source`.
  - If rates can't be fetched, the rate is 0% and the source says "unavailable".
  - `BacktestConfig.cash_interest=False` restores the old behaviour.
- **F4 (walk-forward and regimes).**
  - `backtest/regimes.py`: each day is labelled by the benchmark's state at the previous close (above/below
    its 200-day average; 21-day volatility above/below its trailing 1-year median). Each experiment stores
    days %, strategy and benchmark return per year, and Sharpe above cash per regime.
  - `backtest/walkforward.py`: in each fold, every candidate value is scored on ~3 years in-sample
    (Sharpe above cash) and the winner is traded on the next ~6 months. Out-of-sample folds are stitched.
  - It uses train + validation data only and never the test period. Every candidate's in-sample score is
    stored. Daily strategies only.
  - Available in the CLI (`walkforward`) and as a Strategy Lab run mode. Reports treat walk-forward as
    out-of-sample evidence.
- **F7 ORB stop.**
  - Strategies may now return an exact `stop_price`. ORB sets it to the range low or midpoint, so the stop
    no longer drifts with a gap-up fill.
  - If the next open is already at or below the stop, there's no trade.
- **Trials** now count distinct parameter sets: re-running an identical configuration isn't a new try.
- **Tests:** 12 new (`test_audit_fixes_4_7.py`); tests never fetch rates (`QE_CASH_RATE=zero` in the fixture).
  Suite: **485 passed**.
